# Match Research Guide

## Research Goals

Every puzzle should be based on a match with enough reliable information to support fair guessing.

Minimum useful data:

- Match date.
- Teams.
- Competition.
- Venue.
- Final score.
- Starting lineups.
- Substitutes if used in the puzzle.
- Managers when relevant.
- Source links.

## Source Preferences

Prefer primary or highly reputable sources:

- Official club sites.
- Competition sites.
- National federation sites.
- Trusted databases with clear match records.
- Contemporary match reports when available.

Avoid relying on a single unsourced page for lineups when the match is likely to be contentious or famous.

## Candidate Match Criteria

A good puzzle match usually has at least one of:

- Major tournament knockout match.
- Title-deciding league match.
- Famous comeback, upset, derby, final, or debut.
- Iconic lineup with a few memorable missing names.
- Strong cultural memory among football fans.

## Data Quality Notes

Record uncertainty explicitly:

- Conflicting spellings.
- Shirt number uncertainty.
- Position ambiguity.
- Substitution timing disagreement.
- Player name changes or transliteration variants.

## Player Alias Rules

Track common accepted names:

- Full name.
- Common short name.
- Accented and unaccented forms.
- Transliteration variants.
- Known mononyms.
- Initials only when widely used.
